Building a good financial plan for one person is difficult. An effective plan must provide for tomorrow while also covering today, protecting against risks, and helping the investor stay disciplined along the way.

Building a financial plan for two people (a couple) who see money differently is much more difficult. In most cases, partners have very different views on how to spend, save, and pass on money.

From working with many couples over the years, we are more convinced than ever that personal finance is more personal than it is finance.

## Where Each Story Comes From

A person’s money story often starts with their earliest childhood memories, long before they had any money of their own. How money was talked about and experienced in their youth teaches lessons that become hard to change or update.

Those early lessons stay with a person for decades, and they shape how each partner reacts to financial decisions.

When two partners have had very different experiences with money, and therefore learnt different lessons and habits, it can have very real consequences for how they handle money together.

One partner may remember a parent losing their job, and how quickly the household had to reduce spending. Saving money for a rainy day will forever appeal to them. For the other partner, the memory might be parents who saved for years and had little time to enjoy it, or a parent who took a chance that paid off. Playing it safe will never look sensible to them.

## When One Story Dominates

Understanding how deep-rooted these memories and habits can be means that building a shared plan that caters for both mindsets is very difficult.

We’ve noticed that over time, most couples settle into roles. Often one partner looks after the money and does most of the talking about it. This can result in a financial plan that caters only to that partner’s views on money.

Once that happens, the partner whose view keeps losing out may start to see themselves as the problem. Unless this partner can see some of their own goals in the plan, it’s not truly a shared plan.

The stories behind each partner’s money views will never change, but by trying to find room for both views, it becomes possible for shared priorities, rather than disagreements, to remain at the centre.

## A Shared Plan

When building a financial plan for couples, we try our best to understand both money stories before we look at the numbers.

Often, what seems like a disagreement turns out to be two people who come from different places, having learnt different lessons for different reasons.

Our job is to create a plan that gives your family the best chance of financial independence. That includes making sure that the plan works for everyone in the family, no matter what money scars they carry.

If this challenge sounds familiar, we encourage you to bring both money stories to your next planning meeting, and we can look at how your plan makes room for each of them.
